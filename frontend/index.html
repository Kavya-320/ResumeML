Pipeline

How It Works

Nine automated steps from raw resume files to ranked candidates

📁
Step 01

Upload Resumes

Add files individually or select a whole folder. PDF, DOCX, TXT and image resumes are supported.

📝
Step 02

Enter Job Description

Paste the role, required skills and years of experience once for the whole batch.

🛡️
Step 03

Validate Files

Extension, content type, file signature and size are checked. Invalid files are reported without stopping the batch.

📄
Step 04

Text Extraction

PyMuPDF for PDFs, python-docx for DOCX. OCR with Tesseract runs only for scanned PDFs and images.

🧠
Step 05

Generate Embeddings

A pretrained language model converts overlapping parts of the resume and job description into semantic vectors.

📐
Step 06

Semantic Similarity

Cosine similarity of normalized vectors, calibrated into a 0–1 fit value with a fixed, documented range.

🔎
Step 07

Skill Matching

A curated skill catalog with aliases finds required skills in the job description and checks each resume.

⏱️
Step 08

Experience Extraction

Employment date ranges are merged without double counting; stated years are used otherwise. Unknown stays unknown.

🏆
Step 09

Score & Rank

Weighted score of 55% semantic, 35% skills, 10% experience, always within 0–100, then ranked.

About

About

A recruitment screening assistant that helps you organise resumes and compare them against a job description.

🎯

What is ResumeML?

ResumeML reads a batch of resumes, compares each one with your job description and ranks the candidates by how closely they match. You see the evidence behind every ranking: matched skills, missing skills, experience and email.

💡

Why it was built

Reading hundreds of resumes by hand is slow and inconsistent. ResumeML gives every resume the same first-pass comparison, so recruiters can spend their time on the candidates who look most relevant.

⚖️

What the score means

The match score runs from 0 to 100 and shows how well a resume fits the job description. It supports screening, it is not a hiring decision, and it is not a probability of being hired. People should always review the results.
